## Outcomes of the timing measures

Each timing measure gives, per cell and period, a date or one of these reasons (the code is what `timing.nc` stores):

| code | outcome |
|---|---|
| 0 | a date |
| 1 | not estimable: records missing (a day of the period has no record in any cell) |
| 2 | not estimable: the period starts before the run's records (for snow_peak: the peak may lie before the record starts) |
| 3 | no snow |
| 4 | snow persists |
| 5 | no leaves |
| 6 | leaf area changes too little for a green-up date |
| 7 | no dormant season |
| 8 | no photosynthesis |
| 9 | no runoff: the water year's total is not above zero |
| 10 | not estimable: insufficient follow-up (the snow falls below the threshold too close to the end of the days searched to see the full run of days) |
| 11 | not estimable: the cell lacks values on days the records hold |

Quality flags stand beside a date (bits of the flag value):

| bit | flag | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `snow_returns` | snow returns: after the melt-out, snow water stays at or above the threshold for as many consecutive days as a melt-out needs |
| 2 | `partial_record` | partial-record estimate: the water year starts before the run's records; a separate quantity, kept out of normals |
| 4 | `two_peaks` | another peak of the running mean comes within the 'near' share of the largest, with a dip of more than the 'dip' share between the two |
| 8 | `runoff_falls` | the cumulative runoff falls on some day (negative daily runoff), so it may cross the half mark more than once |

Dates are days of the year in the calendar the run's files declare, 1 = the year's first day. A water year is filed under the year it ends, and its dates before the new year count back (in a noleap calendar, 31 December = 0, 1 October = -91). The year starts in January and the water year in October unless the control's registry entry names other months (`year_start_month`, `water_year_start_month`). A running mean is dated by its middle day and uses only windows inside the period; ties go to the earliest day. Region distributions are area-weighted (cell area times land fraction): the median and quartiles of the dates, the counts of dates, of each reason and of each flag (with the flag's area and the denominator of its share), the share of the area whose inputs are complete (missing time records and missing cell values counted apart), the shift in days against the normal years (a cell's normal is the mean of its dates when every normal year gives one) or the control, and, for each alternative setting, the sensitivity record: the setting, the region's median date and median shift beside the default's, and the median per-cell change of date. Every shift and change of date is on the cells with a date in both, and beside it stands the change of eligibility: the cells and area shares with a date in both, in one only, in neither, and the area the statistic covers.

## 1. `anomaly` (version 2, text revision 2)

**Estimates.** A period's value minus the mean of the normal years' values of the same period, for a region or per cell, with the normal years' support.

**Inputs.**

* the suite's region series of the field for the period's kind (year, water year, season, month) (units: the field's units per period; resolution: one value per region and period)
* the suite's maps, for a per-cell anomaly (units: the field's units per period; resolution: per cell, for years, water years and seasons)
* the field notes' type and percent policy (resolution: per field)
* the normal years and the event years (resolution: from the study's plan)

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `normal_exists`: At least one normal year holds the period; else not estimable.
* `normal_period_clean`: A normal year's period that holds a month of an event year (the winter or water year after it) is left out of the normal, with the reason; the count of normal years shown drops.
* `percent_policy`: A percent of the reference only where the field notes' reporting policy allows it (percent.allowed, apart from the physical scale): not for temperatures in any unit, TWS, ZWT, signed or net quantities such as NEE or NPP, or RH2M, whose changes are in percentage points; never for an event or a bounded index; and none for a field whose note has no policy.
* `reference_not_near_zero`: No percent where the reference is zero or its size is below the near-zero share of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind (the twelve months for a month; the four seasons for a season, a year or a water year).

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `near_zero_share` | 0.01 | share of a range | A reference this small beside the field's own range over the periods of its kind makes a percent large without meaning. The yardstick is a range, so it does not depend on where a unit puts its zero. | none | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* the period is absent from the run: the suite holds no value for it; the suite's own reason is given
* not estimable: no normal year has this period: every normal year lacks it or is left out
* no percent, with the reason: the field notes' policy allows none (or the note has no policy), an event, a bounded index, or a near-zero normal

**Outputs.**

* value, normal (the mean of the normal years used), anomaly, percent or the reason for none, in the field's units per period; change_units, the units of the anomaly (percentage points for a field kept in percent)
* support: how many normal years, which, their standard deviation (ddof 1), smallest and largest; the years left out or missing, with reasons
* coverage: the share of the region's area each input value covers (per cell, counted over the vegetated cells), the inputs with missing cell values (a share below 1) and, apart from them, the inputs with missing time records (a period the run lacks); per cell also the result's own share of the area and its cells without a value
* the reference range the near-zero rule used

**Numerical contract.**

* units: the field's units per period (a rate's total over the period, a mean otherwise); the anomaly is in the same units, and a field kept in percent changes in percentage points; the percent is a percent of the normal
* area weights: a region's value is the suite's own, area-weighted by each cell's area times its land fraction (weight_km2); per cell nothing is weighted, and coverage is the share of that area
* precision: maps are float32 in the suite and become float64 here; normals, changes, percents and shares are float64
* tolerances: none: the normal is the plain mean of the normal years used, and a change of zero is exactly zero
* near zero: no percent where the normal is zero or its size is below the near-zero share (0.01) of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind; the absolute anomaly is always given

**Limitations.**

* The normal is the mean of the run's own normal years: it describes this model's variability, not the observed climate's.
* A percent is withheld rather than inflated where the normal is near zero; the absolute anomaly is always given.
* Per-cell monthly values are not available yet: the suite references the monthly history files but has no access path to their per-cell values, so per-cell results are made for years, water years and seasons only.

## 2. `difference` (version 2, text revision 2)

**Estimates.** Run minus control for the same period and region, or per cell, with the percent of the control under the anomaly's rules.

**Inputs.**

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `percent_policy`: A percent of the reference only where the field notes' reporting policy allows it (percent.allowed, apart from the physical scale): not for temperatures in any unit, TWS, ZWT, signed or net quantities such as NEE or NPP, or RH2M, whose changes are in percentage points; never for an event or a bounded index; and none for a field whose note has no policy.
* `reference_not_near_zero`: No percent where the reference is zero or its size is below the near-zero share of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind (the twelve months for a month; the four seasons for a season, a year or a water year).

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `near_zero_share` | 0.01 | share of a range | A reference this small beside the field's own range over the periods of its kind makes a percent large without meaning. The yardstick is a range, so it does not depend on where a unit puts its zero. | none | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* the period is absent from a run: either suite lacks it
* no percent, with the reason: the field notes' policy allows none (or the note has no policy), an event, a bounded index, or a near-zero control value

**Outputs.**

## 3. `snow_peak` (version 3, text revision 3)

**Estimates.** The day of the water year's largest snow water (H2OSNO), per cell.

**Inputs.**

* H2OSNO, daily, from the run's daily history (units: mm; resolution: per cell and day)
* the water year's start month (resolution: the control's registry entry, October when it names none)

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `inputs_complete`: Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records).
* `record_holds_the_start`: The run holds the water year's start; a water year whose first months the run lacks is not estimable in every cell: the peak may lie before the record starts (a largest after the record starts is no proof).
* `holds_snow`: The water year's largest is at least the threshold; else no snow.

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `threshold_mm` | 1 | mm of snow water | The same mark as snow_gone's: below it a cell holds no snowpack to date. | none | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* not estimable: records missing (a day of the period has no record in any cell): a day of the water year has no record
* not estimable: the cell lacks values on days the records hold: the cell lacks a value on a day the records hold
* not estimable: the period starts before the run's records (for snow_peak: the peak may lie before the record starts): the run starts after the water year starts
* no snow: the water year's largest is below the threshold

**Outputs.**

* per cell and water year: the date (days of the year the water year ends in, earlier days counted back: 31 December = 0, 1 October = -91 in a noleap calendar) or the reason; the largest snow water
* per region: the area-weighted median and quartiles of the dates; the counts of dates, of each reason and of each flag, with each flag's area, its share and the denominator of that share (the region's area, the area with a date); the share of the area whose inputs are complete, with missing time records and missing cell values counted apart; the shift in days against the normal years or the control on the cells with a date in both, beside the change of eligibility (cells and area shares with a date in both, only in this year, only in the reference, in neither) and the area the shift covers; the sensitivity record of each alternative setting with its own change of eligibility; and the range across the settings: each evaluated setting's median shift on the comparable cells (those eligible under every evaluated setting, in the year and in its baseline), the comparable cells' count and share of the region's area beside each setting's own eligibility, the default, the smallest and the largest shift each with its setting, whether the largest minus the smallest exceeds 7 days, and whether the settings reverse the direction of the shift (some give an earlier date, others a later one)

**Numerical contract.**

* units: whole days: the day of the year, 1 = its first day, in the calendar the run's files declare; a water-year measure counts the days before 1 January back (31 December = 0); a shift is in days, positive when later
* area weights: region distributions are of equal area: each cell holds its area times its land fraction (weight_km2), and a quantile is the smallest date at which the cumulative share of the area reaches it, the midpoint of two dates where it reaches it exactly
* precision: daily values are float64; a running mean is the mean of each window on its own, so equal windows give equal means; dates are whole days, stored as float32 in timing.nc (exact for whole days); medians and quartiles can fall on half days
* tolerances: none: a mark is passed when a value reaches it, a value is below a threshold when strictly less, and ties go to the earliest day. Across settings: the smallest and the largest shift are of the settings' medians on the comparable cells, a tie keeping the setting listed first (the default, then the alternatives in the contract's order); the range exceeds 7 days when the largest minus the smallest is more than 7 days by over 1e-9 of a day; a median within 1e-9 of a day of zero is neither earlier nor later; with no comparable cell there is no range
* near zero: a largest below the threshold is no snow, never a date; a missing value is never taken for zero

**Limitations.**

* The date of a single day's largest: a long flat peak is dated by its first day (ties go to the earliest day).
* A date says when the model's snowpack peaked, not why.

## 4. `snow_gone` (version 3, text revision 3)

**Estimates.** The first day of the first sustained melt-out after the water year's snow peak: the first of the run of days on which snow water stays below the threshold, per cell. It marks the end of the main snowpack, not the final disappearance of snow, which a later snowfall can postpone.

**Inputs.**

* H2OSNO, daily, from the run's daily history (units: mm; resolution: per cell and day)
* the water year's start month (resolution: the control's registry entry, October when it names none)

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `inputs_complete`: Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records).
* `holds_snow`: The water year's largest is at least the threshold; else no snow.

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `threshold_mm` | 1 | mm of snow water | It marks snow present or gone whatever the size of the snowpack, the same in a thin snowpack and a deep one. | 5 | approved for integration |
| `days` | 14 | days | Long enough that a short warm spell inside the snow season is not taken for its end. | 7 | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* not estimable: records missing (a day of the period has no record in any cell): a day of the water year has no record
* not estimable: the cell lacks values on days the records hold: the cell lacks a value on a day the records hold
* no snow: the water year's largest is below the threshold
* not estimable: insufficient follow-up (the snow falls below the threshold too close to the end of the days searched to see the full run of days): days below the threshold start after the peak but reach the end of the days searched (the water year's end) before the run is long enough: the melt-out may have begun, but its full run cannot be seen
* snow persists: no run of days below the threshold starts after the peak, and none reaches the end of the water year: the snow stays
* snow returns: after the melt-out, snow water stays at or above the threshold for as many consecutive days as a melt-out needs: a flag beside the date
* partial-record estimate: the water year starts before the run's records; a separate quantity, kept out of normals: a flag on every cell of a water year that starts before the run's records: the search starts at the largest of the months the run holds

**Outputs.**

* per cell and water year: the date (as snow_peak's) or the reason, and the flags
* per region: the area-weighted median and quartiles of the dates; the counts of dates, of each reason and of each flag, with each flag's area, its share and the denominator of that share (the region's area, the area with a date); the share of the area whose inputs are complete, with missing time records and missing cell values counted apart; the shift in days against the normal years or the control on the cells with a date in both, beside the change of eligibility (cells and area shares with a date in both, only in this year, only in the reference, in neither) and the area the shift covers; the sensitivity record of each alternative setting with its own change of eligibility; and the range across the settings: each evaluated setting's median shift on the comparable cells (those eligible under every evaluated setting, in the year and in its baseline), the comparable cells' count and share of the region's area beside each setting's own eligibility, the default, the smallest and the largest shift each with its setting, whether the largest minus the smallest exceeds 7 days, and whether the settings reverse the direction of the shift (some give an earlier date, others a later one)

**Numerical contract.**

* units: whole days: the day of the year, 1 = its first day, in the calendar the run's files declare; a water-year measure counts the days before 1 January back (31 December = 0); a shift is in days, positive when later
* area weights: region distributions are of equal area: each cell holds its area times its land fraction (weight_km2), and a quantile is the smallest date at which the cumulative share of the area reaches it, the midpoint of two dates where it reaches it exactly
* precision: daily values are float64; a running mean is the mean of each window on its own, so equal windows give equal means; dates are whole days, stored as float32 in timing.nc (exact for whole days); medians and quartiles can fall on half days
* tolerances: none: a mark is passed when a value reaches it, a value is below a threshold when strictly less, and ties go to the earliest day. Across settings: the smallest and the largest shift are of the settings' medians on the comparable cells, a tie keeping the setting listed first (the default, then the alternatives in the contract's order); the range exceeds 7 days when the largest minus the smallest is more than 7 days by over 1e-9 of a day; a median within 1e-9 of a day of zero is neither earlier nor later; with no comparable cell there is no range
* near zero: snow water strictly below the threshold counts as gone; a largest below the threshold is no snow, never a date

**Limitations.**

* Searched from the peak, not from a date, so a mid-winter melt before the main snowpack is not taken for the season's end; a second, later snowpack is flagged, not dated.
* The first sustained melt-out, not the final disappearance of snow: snow that returns later in the water year is flagged snow returns, and its later melt-out is not dated.
* The run of days must end inside the water year, so a water year's date never depends on the next one; a melt-out that begins in the water year's last days is insufficient follow-up, apart from snow that persists. A record that ends before the water year does leaves the water year with records missing.
* A partial-record date is a separate quantity: it never enters a normal, and a shift made with one is labelled restricted comparability.
* Its shift can move with the setting: when the largest minus the smallest shift across the settings exceeds 7 days, a claim giving the shift gives the default, the smallest and the largest, each with its setting, and a claim on a result whose settings reverse the direction of the shift carries that warning. The range is on the comparable cells, so it can differ from each setting's own shift, which is on that setting's eligible cells.

## 9. `screen` (version 2, text revision 2)

**Estimates.** For every catalog field and every period of one year (the year, its water year, its seasons and months) over one region, the anomaly against the normal years or the difference from the control, as one complete table scored by variable type.

**Inputs.**

* the suite's region series of every field (units: the field's units per period; resolution: one value per region and period)
* the field notes' process group, variable type, scale and percent policy (resolution: per field)
* the normal years (and, for an experiment, the control's suite) (resolution: from the study's plan)

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `support`: Two or more normal values, none of them short of the region's area (missing cell values); else insufficient support and no score. Normal years without the period (missing time records) lower the count shown.
* `near_constant`: A normal whose standard deviation is zero or at most the near-constant share of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind (for a year or water-year row, over the four seasons) is flagged and gets no score, never a large one.
* `no_change`: A change of exactly zero is marked no change and gets no score.
* `percent_policy`: A percent of the reference only where the field notes' reporting policy allows it (percent.allowed, apart from the physical scale): not for temperatures in any unit, TWS, ZWT, signed or net quantities such as NEE or NPP, or RH2M, whose changes are in percentage points; never for an event or a bounded index; and none for a field whose note has no policy.

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `near_constant_share` | 0.01 | share of a range | A normal with almost no spread would turn any change into a huge score; the yardstick is a range, so it does not depend on where a unit puts its zero. | none | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* scored: fluxes and states: the change over the normal years' standard deviation (ddof 1); for an experiment, the run minus the control over the control's normal-year standard deviation for that period
* marked above_normal_maximum: an event row (burned area in km2, carbon lost to fire) above the normal years' largest
* marked differs_from_control: an experiment's event row unlike the control's
* within the normal years' range: an event row that is no larger than in a normal year: it stays in the table, unmarked
* not scored yet: stores and bounded indices: their absolute and relative change stay in the table
* no change: the change is exactly zero
* near-constant normal: flagged, never scored
* insufficient support: fewer than two normal values or a normal value short of the region's area
* period absent: the run lacks the period; the suite's reason is given

**Outputs.**

* the table: one row per field and period with the value, the reference, the absolute change and its units (change_units: percentage points for a field kept in percent), the percent where the field notes' policy allows it (the anomaly's rules), the score where its type has one, the normal years' support (count, spread, near-constant flag), the field's group, type and scale, the share of the region's area the value covers, and two columns kept apart: missing cell values (a value, the control's or a normal year's short of the region's area) and missing time records (an absent period, normal years without it); written whole as a CSV
* the display rule, written beside the table, in full: Shown first, per process group, the groups in the model's order (the catalog's): the three rows with the largest absolute score, largest first, at most one per field (its highest-scored period; its other periods stay in the table); a tie keeps the field that comes first in the catalog and, within a field, the period that comes first in the table (the year, the water year, the seasons, the months); a group without a scored row shows none. Then every event row marked above_normal_maximum or differs_from_control, in the table's order. Then, for each field of a type not scored yet (stores, bounded indices), its largest absolute change with its period, labelled not scored, a tie keeping the earlier period. Every score is shown with the number of normal years behind it. Rows without a score (near-constant normal, insufficient support, no change, period absent) are not shown first and stay in the table. The whole table is the CSV beside this rule.
* the rows the display rule shows first

**Numerical contract.**

* units: a row's value, reference and change are in the field's units per period (burned area in km2); a field kept in percent changes in percentage points; a score has no unit (a change over a standard deviation)
* area weights: the suite's region values, area-weighted as the suite makes them (cell area times land fraction); the screen weights nothing itself
* precision: maps are float32 in the suite and become float64 here; normals, changes, percents and shares are float64
* tolerances: a change of exactly zero is no change; there is no other tolerance
* near zero: a normal whose standard deviation is zero or at most the near-constant share (0.01) of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind gets no score; no percent where the reference is near zero (the anomaly's rule)

**Limitations.**

* A score compares a change with the normal years' spread in this run; it is never compared across variable types.
* Stores (to be scored by their drift against the normal years' trend) and bounded indices are not scored in this build.
* What the screen turns up was not predicted: it is exploratory, never a tested expectation.

## 10. `overlap` (version 2, text revision 2)

**Estimates.** How two per-cell maps go together over a region: their area-weighted correlation across cells, and the first map's mean in each quarter of the region's area ranked by the second.

**Inputs.**

* two maps on the same grid (units: each map's own units; resolution: per cell)
* the region's mask, the cell weights and the elevation bands (units: km2; resolution: per cell, from the suite's masks)

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `enough_cells`: At least three cells hold both maps; else no correlation.
* `sign_share`: The share of the area agreeing in sign is reported only when neither sign of either map covers more than the sign-share limit of the area.

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sign_share_limit` | 0.8 | share of the area | When one sign covers most of the area, agreement in sign is high by chance and says little. | none | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* no correlation: fewer than three cells, or a map constant over them
* no same-sign share: one sign covers more than the limit

**Outputs.**

* the correlation, the quarters (their area, the second map's range, the first map's mean), the sign shares, the same-sign share or why none; the same by band; the share of the region's area both maps cover, and each map's cells without a value (missing cell values) with their share of the area; missing time records do not apply, since each map is one period

**Numerical contract.**

* units: the correlation has no unit; quarter means are in the first map's units; shares are of the area
* area weights: every statistic weights a cell by its area times its land fraction; the quarters hold equal areas exactly, a cell across a quarter's edge split between the two
* precision: float64 throughout
* tolerances: none: the signs are taken against zero exactly
* near zero: a map constant over the cells gives no correlation; a value of exactly zero has no sign

**Limitations.**

* An association across cells, never a cause: two maps can go together because both follow a third.

## 11. `elevation_split` (version 2, text revision 2)

**Estimates.** One map summarised by the study's elevation bands and by each other registered region and its complement, within the requested region: where the change sits and how concentrated it is.

**Inputs.**

* a map on the grid (units: its own units; resolution: per cell)
* the requested region (resolution: the suite's mask of it)
* the elevation bands (units: m; resolution: the control's registry edges, as the suite's band masks; a control without edges gets them every 500 m across its elevations, a fallback recorded in its entry)
* the other registered regions (resolution: the suite's masks)

**Applies where.** Checked by code, per cell and period:

* `has_bands`: The suite records band edges and their masks.
* `within_the_region`: Every band and every other region is cut to the requested region; a band with no cell of the region is flagged empty, and one under the small-part share of the region's area flagged small.

**Settings.**

| setting | default | units | why this default | alternatives computed beside it | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `small_share` | 0.01 | share of the region's area | A band this small holds too few cells for its mean and shares to say much; it is flagged, not dropped. | none | approved for integration |

**Outcomes and quality checks.**

* an empty band: no cell of the requested region lies in it: flagged empty, zero cells
* a small band: under the small-part share of the region's area: flagged small, its values kept

**Outputs.**

* per band and part, within the region: its cells in the region and with a value, its share of the region's area, the empty and small flags, its cells without a value (missing cell values), the area with a value and its share, the mean, the positive and negative contributions (change times area), the share of the total absolute change, and the concentration within it
* for the region: its name, cells and area, the share its values cover, its cells without a value, the mean, the concentration (the smallest share of the area holding half of the total absolute change) and the flagged bands; missing time records do not apply, since the map is one period

**Numerical contract.**

* units: band means in the map's units; contributions in the map's units times km2; shares of the area or of the total absolute change have no unit
* area weights: each cell weighs its area times its land fraction; shares of the area are of the region's area with a value, and the flags use the band's share of the region's whole area
* precision: float64 throughout
* tolerances: a band under the small-part share (0.01) of the region's area is flagged small; there is no other tolerance
* near zero: a total absolute change of zero gives no shares of it and no concentration

**Limitations.**

* A share of a net change that nearly cancels would mislead, so shares are of the absolute change and stand beside the share of the area.
* The 500 m step for a control without edges is a fallback, not a scientific partition. Edges are set in the registry before any result is seen and must not be chosen after seeing results.

### `snow_water_to_next_year_leaves` (text revision 2)

snow to melt to soil water to BTRAN, which acts on both transpiration and GPP, with GPP carried over to next year's leaf carbon only under conditions.

**Fields:** `H2OSNO`, `QSNOMELT`, `H2OSOI`, `BTRAN`, `QVEGT`, `GPP`, `LEAFC`.

**Links** (explicit, one step each; a field can act on more than one other, and each link holds only under its conditions):

* `H2OSNO` to `QSNOMELT`: the snowpack melts as it warms; a smaller or earlier-melting snowpack gives less melt, or the same melt earlier. Holds when: a seasonal snowpack forms in the cell.
* `QSNOMELT` to `H2OSOI`: melt water infiltrates and refills the soil before the growing season. Holds when: the soil is not frozen or already full when the melt comes, so the melt is not lost as runoff.
* `H2OSOI` to `BTRAN`: the transpiration factor falls as the root zone dries, weighted by where the roots are. Holds when: the root zone dries far enough in the growing season to lower BTRAN below 1.
* `BTRAN` to `QVEGT`: a lower factor closes the stomata: less transpiration. Holds when: the canopy has leaves to transpire through.
* `BTRAN` to `GPP`: the same stomatal closure lowers photosynthesis. Holds when: light, temperature, nitrogen or leaf area do not limit photosynthesis first in the same weeks.
* `GPP` to `LEAFC` (conditional carry-over): carbon fixed this year can reach next year's leaves through the plants' allocation and storage, so an effect can carry over a year. Holds when: the model allocates part of the new carbon to leaves, or to storage that feeds next year's leaves, rather than to stems and roots; the plants store carbon and draw on it the next year; leaves turn over slowly enough, or are grown anew from stored carbon, for this year's carbon to reach next year's leaves; the vegetation type sets the path: an evergreen canopy keeps its leaves, a deciduous one grows them anew; no disturbance (fire, mortality) removes the plants in between.

**Expected when:**

* the snowpack is the main source of the growing season's soil water, and summer rain is small beside it
* the root-zone soil water limits transpiration in the growing season (BTRAN below 1 for a lasting part of it)

**Competing mechanisms:**

* summer rain refills the soil and breaks the link from snow to soil water (`RAIN`, `H2OSOI`)
* warmer, drier air or more sunshine changes photosynthesis and transpiration without a change in soil water (`TBOT`, `RH2M`, `FSDS`)
* nitrogen limits photosynthesis whatever the water (`FPG`)
* a change in leaf area (fire, a previous dry year) changes transpiration and photosynthesis directly (`TLAI`, `FAREA_BURNED`)
* the plants' stored carbon buffers next year's leaves, so leaf carbon need not follow this year's GPP (`LEAFC`, `TOTVEGC`)

**Limits of inference:**

* A run of the model shows the model's own mechanism, not nature's: agreement in sign and timing is consistent with the links, not a proof of them.
* BTRAN acts on transpiration and on GPP at once: a change in both is one cause with two effects, not a chain from transpiration to GPP.
* The carry-over from GPP to next year's leaf carbon is conditional: without the allocation, storage and turnover of the cells concerned, a link between one year's GPP and the next year's LEAFC is an association.
* Attribution within the model needs a run that changes one link and nothing else (for example the weather of one season); without it the finding is an association.
* Soil water is read as the top-1 m mean of H2OSOI; roots deeper than a metre draw on water this does not show.

**Methods that can give evidence:** `snow_peak`, `snow_gone`, `anomaly`, `difference`, `growth_peak`, `overlap`, `elevation_split`.

### `temperature_to_heterotrophic_respiration` (text revision 2)

temperature to soil temperature to heterotrophic respiration.

**Fields:** `TSA`, `TSOI_10CM`, `HR`.

**Links** (explicit, one step each; a field can act on more than one other, and each link holds only under its conditions):

* `TSA` to `TSOI_10CM`: the air warms the soil, damped and delayed with depth. Holds when: no deep snow insulates the soil, which holds it near freezing.
* `TSOI_10CM` to `HR`: decomposition of litter and soil organic matter speeds up as the soil warms. Holds when: the soil is not frozen; the soil is neither so dry nor so wet that water limits decomposition.

**Expected when:**

* the litter and soil carbon pools are not themselves changing fast (after a fire or a large litterfall)

**Competing mechanisms:**

* soil water limits decomposition in a dry period, so a warm, dry spell can lower heterotrophic respiration (`H2OSOI`, `SOILWATER_10CM`)
* the size of the litter and soil carbon pools sets how much there is to decompose (`TOTLITC`, `TOTSOMC`, `CWDC`)
* nitrogen limits the decomposers' immobilization (`FPI`, `SMINN`)
* a fire adds or removes litter (`COL_FIRE_CLOSS`)

**Limits of inference:**

* Across cells, temperature follows elevation, and so do soil carbon and soil water: a correlation across cells mixes all three.
* A warm year is often a dry one: within the model, only a run that changes temperature alone separates the two.

**Methods that can give evidence:** `anomaly`, `difference`, `overlap`, `elevation_split`, `screen`.

### `nitrogen_to_gpp` (text revision 4)

nitrogen (soil mineral nitrogen and FPG) to GPP, in a run whose verified configuration leaves nitrogen as the only nutrient limit.

**Fields:** `SMINN`, `FPG`, `GPP`.

**Links** (explicit, one step each; a field can act on more than one other, and each link holds only under its conditions):

* `SMINN` to `FPG`: less mineral nitrogen beside the plants' demand lowers FPG, the fraction of potential GPP the available nitrogen allows. Holds when: the run cycles nitrogen and does not supplement it (suplnitro = 'NONE'), as in the verified configuration.
* `FPG` to `GPP`: the model lowers photosynthesis to the carbon the available nitrogen can support. Holds when: only nitrogen can limit growth by nutrients: in the verified configuration the model represents phosphorus limitation, but phosphorus is supplemented to meet plant demand everywhere (suplphos = 'ALL'), which switches it off (FPG_P, the matching fraction for phosphorus in the monthly output, was exactly 1 in every cell and month of 2015-2023 in the Naches control run, checked on 2026-11-05: 160,728 finite cell-months a year), so nitrogen and phosphorus cannot co-limit; in a run without the phosphorus supplement, phosphorus can limit too, alone or with nitrogen, and FPG alone does not show the nutrient limit: FPG and FPG_P are needed together to tell nitrogen limitation from phosphorus limitation.

**Expected when:**

* the run's nutrient configuration is the verified one (carbon, nitrogen and phosphorus cycles; phosphorus supplemented everywhere, which switches phosphorus limitation off; nitrogen not supplemented), read from the control's facts cache, key configuration, and shown in the catalog
* FPG is below 1 in the growing season, so nitrogen limits potential GPP

**Competing mechanisms:**

* the plants' demand changes (more or less potential photosynthesis) and moves FPG without a change in supply (`PLANT_NDEMAND`)
* phosphorus limits GPP, alone or together with nitrogen; this cannot happen in the verified configuration, where the supplement (suplphos = 'ALL') switches phosphorus limitation off and FPG_P, in the monthly output, was exactly 1 in every cell and month of 2015-2023 in the Naches control run (checked on 2026-11-05: 160,728 finite cell-months a year); in a run without the supplement, FPG and FPG_P together tell the two apart (`FPG`)
* water stress limits GPP separately from nutrients, through BTRAN, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (`BTRAN`)
* temperature limits GPP separately from nutrients, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (`TSA`)
* light limits GPP separately from nutrients, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (`FSDS`)
* leaf area limits GPP separately from nutrients, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (`TLAI`)
* a fire releases nitrogen or removes the plants that take it up (`COL_FIRE_CLOSS`, `FAREA_BURNED`)

**Limits of inference:**

* In the verified configuration FPG measures only the nitrogen limitation of potential GPP, and it shows that limit at the time: FPG below 1 says nitrogen limits potential GPP then, not that it is the only or the main limit. Nitrogen can also lower GPP through earlier effects that FPG at the time does not show (less leaf area or leaf nitrogen grown in a nitrogen-poor period), so an unchanged FPG rules out only a same-time nitrogen limit.
* FPG is the model's own downregulation of photosynthesis: it can follow demand as well as supply, so a fall in FPG is not by itself a shortage of soil nitrogen.
* Soil mineral nitrogen is a store: its change over one year mixes uptake, mineralization, deposition and losses.
* A run configured otherwise (phosphorus not supplemented, or nitrogen supplemented) needs its own reading of FPG: without the phosphorus supplement, FPG and FPG_P (in the monthly output) are needed together to tell nitrogen limitation from phosphorus limitation.

**Methods that can give evidence:** `anomaly`, `difference`, `overlap`, `screen`.

### `burned_area_to_transpiration` (text revision 2)

burned area to carbon lost to fire to leaf area to transpiration.

**Fields:** `FAREA_BURNED`, `COL_FIRE_CLOSS`, `TLAI`, `QVEGT`.

**Links** (explicit, one step each; a field can act on more than one other, and each link holds only under its conditions):

* the model's background fire burns a little in most periods, spread thin over many cells (`FAREA_BURNED`)
* different weather, not the fire, changes transpiration (`TBOT`, `RH2M`, `FSDS`)

* The fire is the model's own unless the run prescribes burned area.
