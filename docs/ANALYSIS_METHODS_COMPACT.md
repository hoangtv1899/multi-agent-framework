# Analysis methods: the compact view

Generated by `tools/make_methods_doc.py` for the Analyzer from the same module as `docs/ANALYSIS_METHODS.md`, which holds the full contracts (inputs, outcomes, outputs, numerical contract, tests). Do not edit by hand.

Review status: 29 approved for integration, 2 provisional (methods, settings and mechanisms; by the user on 2026-11-05, the user on 2026-11-08); the settings `growth_peak.near` and `growth_peak.dip` approved only as provisional values. This is an approval for integration, not a final scientific approval. Drafts or reviews of an older version: 0.

Each method below is a function in `mcp/elm-mcp/src/domain_methods.py` carrying its contract. Code decides, per cell and period, whether a method applies; where it does not, the outcome is a reason with a count of the cells and periods it covers, never a filled-in value. A setting other than the default runs only when the study's recorded plan names it (or an amendment, whose results count as added after seeing the data); the alternatives a contract names are computed with every result, beside the default. Every result carries the method's id, version and settings.

Picking a method is a choice of what to look at; a method's existence is no reason to report a date or a rank. Sign, timing and overlap support a mechanism and do not establish it.

Timing outcomes, per cell and period: a date, or one of these reason codes: 0 a date; 1 not estimable: records missing (a day of the period has no record in any cell); 2 not estimable: the period starts before the run's records (for snow_peak: the peak may lie before the record starts); 3 no snow; 4 snow persists; 5 no leaves; 6 leaf area changes too little for a green-up date; 7 no dormant season; 8 no photosynthesis; 9 no runoff: the water year's total is not above zero; 10 not estimable: insufficient follow-up (the snow falls below the threshold too close to the end of the days searched to see the full run of days); 11 not estimable: the cell lacks values on days the records hold. Flags beside a date: `snow_returns` snow returns: after the melt-out, snow water stays at or above the threshold for as many consecutive days as a melt-out needs; `partial_record` partial-record estimate: the water year starts before the run's records; a separate quantity, kept out of normals; `two_peaks` another peak of the running mean comes within the 'near' share of the largest, with a dip of more than the 'dip' share between the two; `runoff_falls` the cumulative runoff falls on some day (negative daily runoff), so it may cross the half mark more than once.

## Methods

### `anomaly` (version 2; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: A period's value minus the mean of the normal years' values of the same period, for a region or per cell, with the normal years' support.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): At least one normal year holds the period; else not estimable. A normal year's period that holds a month of an event year (the winter or water year after it) is left out of the normal, with the reason; the count of normal years shown drops. A percent of the reference only where the field notes' reporting policy allows it (percent.allowed, apart from the physical scale): not for temperatures in any unit, TWS, ZWT, signed or net quantities such as NEE or NPP, or RH2M, whose changes are in percentage points; never for an event or a bounded index; and none for a field whose note has no policy. No percent where the reference is zero or its size is below the near-zero share of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind (the twelve months for a month; the four seasons for a season, a year or a water year).
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `near_zero_share` = 0.01 (share of a range); alternatives: none
Limitations: The normal is the mean of the run's own normal years: it describes this model's variability, not the observed climate's. A percent is withheld rather than inflated where the normal is near zero; the absolute anomaly is always given. Per-cell monthly values are not available yet: the suite references the monthly history files but has no access path to their per-cell values, so per-cell results are made for years, water years and seasons only.

### `difference` (version 2; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: Run minus control for the same period and region, or per cell, with the percent of the control under the anomaly's rules.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): A percent of the reference only where the field notes' reporting policy allows it (percent.allowed, apart from the physical scale): not for temperatures in any unit, TWS, ZWT, signed or net quantities such as NEE or NPP, or RH2M, whose changes are in percentage points; never for an event or a bounded index; and none for a field whose note has no policy. No percent where the reference is zero or its size is below the near-zero share of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind (the twelve months for a month; the four seasons for a season, a year or a water year).
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `near_zero_share` = 0.01 (share of a range); alternatives: none
Limitations: A difference within one year says nothing about how unusual it is; the screen sets it against the control's normal-year spread. Per-cell monthly values are not available yet: the suite references the monthly history files but has no access path to their per-cell values, so per-cell results are made for years, water years and seasons only.

### `snow_peak` (version 3; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: The day of the water year's largest snow water (H2OSNO), per cell.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records). The run holds the water year's start; a water year whose first months the run lacks is not estimable in every cell: the peak may lie before the record starts (a largest after the record starts is no proof). The water year's largest is at least the threshold; else no snow.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `threshold_mm` = 1 (mm of snow water); alternatives: none
Limitations: The date of a single day's largest: a long flat peak is dated by its first day (ties go to the earliest day). A date says when the model's snowpack peaked, not why.

### `snow_gone` (version 3; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: The first day of the first sustained melt-out after the water year's snow peak: the first of the run of days on which snow water stays below the threshold, per cell. It marks the end of the main snowpack, not the final disappearance of snow, which a later snowfall can postpone.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records). The water year's largest is at least the threshold; else no snow.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `threshold_mm` = 1 (mm of snow water); alternatives: 5
- `days` = 14 (days); alternatives: 7
Limitations: Searched from the peak, not from a date, so a mid-winter melt before the main snowpack is not taken for the season's end; a second, later snowpack is flagged, not dated. The first sustained melt-out, not the final disappearance of snow: snow that returns later in the water year is flagged snow returns, and its later melt-out is not dated. The run of days must end inside the water year, so a water year's date never depends on the next one; a melt-out that begins in the water year's last days is insufficient follow-up, apart from snow that persists. A record that ends before the water year does leaves the water year with records missing. A partial-record date is a separate quantity: it never enters a normal, and a shift made with one is labelled restricted comparability. Its shift can move with the setting: when the largest minus the smallest shift across the settings exceeds 7 days, a claim giving the shift gives the default, the smallest and the largest, each with its setting, and a claim on a result whose settings reverse the direction of the shift carries that warning. The range is on the comparable cells, so it can differ from each setting's own shift, which is on that setting's eligible cells.

### `leaf_green_up` (version 3; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: The first day leaf area (TLAI) passes a fraction of the way from its low before the yearly peak to that peak, per cell.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records). Leaf area is above zero on some day; else no leaves. The rise from the low before the peak to the peak is at least the rise mark of the peak: an eligibility rule on the year's seasonal amplitude, checked per cell and year; else leaf area changes too little for a green-up date. It does not diagnose the vegetation type or how often leaves turn over.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `fraction` = 0.2 (share of the rise); alternatives: 0.1, 0.5
- `rise_mark` = 0.5 (share of the peak); alternatives: 0.25, 0.75
Limitations: Leaf area, not photosynthesis: kept apart from photosynthesis_onset, never merged into one map or column. The rise mark is an amplitude rule, not a map of vegetation: a cell without a date is a cell whose leaf area changes little within the year, whatever its vegetation, and the eligible area moves with the mark. Its shift can move with the setting: when the largest minus the smallest shift across the settings exceeds 7 days, a claim giving the shift gives the default, the smallest and the largest, each with its setting, and a claim on a result whose settings reverse the direction of the shift carries that warning. The range is on the comparable cells, so it can differ from each setting's own shift, which is on that setting's eligible cells.

### `photosynthesis_onset` (version 3; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: The first day, after the lowest running-mean GPP before the year's peak, on which the running mean passes a fraction of that peak, per cell: the first crossing of the mark, not a sustained onset.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records). The year's peak running mean is above zero; else no photosynthesis. The low before the peak is below the mark (a dormant season); else no dormant season. A watershed whose dormant season falls mid-year names a year that starts in another month in its registry entry.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `fraction` = 0.2 (share of the peak); alternatives: 0.1, 0.3
- `window` = 7 (days); alternatives: none
Limitations: It measures photosynthetic activity, not leaf-out, and responds to light, temperature and water as well as to leaves; kept apart from leaf_green_up. The first crossing of the mark, not a sustained onset: a short early pulse of photosynthesis that crosses the mark and falls back sets the date. With several growing seasons in a year, only the rise to the year's largest peak is dated, from the lowest running mean before it. Its shift can move with the setting: when the largest minus the smallest shift across the settings exceeds 7 days, a claim giving the shift gives the default, the smallest and the largest, each with its setting, and a claim on a result whose settings reverse the direction of the shift carries that warning. The range is on the comparable cells, so it can differ from each setting's own shift, which is on that setting's eligible cells.

### `growth_peak` (version 3; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: The day of the largest running-mean GPP of the year, per cell, flagged where a second peak comes close.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records). The largest running mean is above zero; else no photosynthesis (a largest of nothing is no date).
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `window` = 7 (days); alternatives: 15
- `near` = 0.1 (share of the largest); alternatives: none; PROVISIONAL
- `dip` = 0.1 (share of the largest); alternatives: none; PROVISIONAL
Limitations: Where two peaks are flagged the date can jump between them from year to year; a shift in such cells measures which peak won, not a change of season. The two-peak settings (near, dip) are provisional until representative GPP curves are reviewed; the flag's area is reported with its denominator, not only its count of cells. Its shift can move with the setting: when the largest minus the smallest shift across the settings exceeds 7 days, a claim giving the shift gives the default, the smallest and the largest, each with its setting, and a claim on a result whose settings reverse the direction of the shift carries that warning. The range is on the comparable cells, so it can differ from each setting's own shift, which is on that setting's eligible cells.

### `half_runoff` (version 3; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: The first day on which the water year's cumulative runoff (QRUNOFF) reaches half of its total, per cell.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): The run holds the water year's start; else not estimable in every cell. Every day of the period has a record, else not estimable: records missing, in every cell (a missing time record); and the cell has a value on each of those days, else not estimable: the cell lacks values (missing cell values, kept apart from missing records). The water year's total is above zero; else no runoff.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `fraction` = 0.5 (share of the total); alternatives: none
Limitations: Daily runoff can be negative, so the cumulative curve can cross the half mark more than once: the first crossing is the date, and the cell is flagged. A cell's runoff is the water leaving that cell's land, not routed: it is not the river at a gauge.

### `screen` (version 2; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: For every catalog field and every period of one year (the year, its water year, its seasons and months) over one region, the anomaly against the normal years or the difference from the control, as one complete table scored by variable type.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): Two or more normal values, none of them short of the region's area (missing cell values); else insufficient support and no score. Normal years without the period (missing time records) lower the count shown. A normal whose standard deviation is zero or at most the near-constant share of the range of the field's normals over the periods of its kind (for a year or water-year row, over the four seasons) is flagged and gets no score, never a large one. A change of exactly zero is marked no change and gets no score. A percent of the reference only where the field notes' reporting policy allows it (percent.allowed, apart from the physical scale): not for temperatures in any unit, TWS, ZWT, signed or net quantities such as NEE or NPP, or RH2M, whose changes are in percentage points; never for an event or a bounded index; and none for a field whose note has no policy.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `near_constant_share` = 0.01 (share of a range); alternatives: none
Limitations: A score compares a change with the normal years' spread in this run; it is never compared across variable types. Stores (to be scored by their drift against the normal years' trend) and bounded indices are not scored in this build. What the screen turns up was not predicted: it is exploratory, never a tested expectation.

### `overlap` (version 2; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: How two per-cell maps go together over a region: their area-weighted correlation across cells, and the first map's mean in each quarter of the region's area ranked by the second.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): At least three cells hold both maps; else no correlation. The share of the area agreeing in sign is reported only when neither sign of either map covers more than the sign-share limit of the area.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `sign_share_limit` = 0.8 (share of the area); alternatives: none
Limitations: An association across cells, never a cause: two maps can go together because both follow a third.

### `elevation_split` (version 2; review: approved for integration)
Estimates: One map summarised by the study's elevation bands and by each other registered region and its complement, within the requested region: where the change sits and how concentrated it is.
Applies where (checked by code per cell and period): The suite records band edges and their masks. Every band and every other region is cut to the requested region; a band with no cell of the region is flagged empty, and one under the small-part share of the region's area flagged small.
Settings (the default; the alternatives are computed beside it):
- `small_share` = 0.01 (share of the region's area); alternatives: none
Limitations: A share of a net change that nearly cancels would mislead, so shares are of the absolute change and stand beside the share of the area. The 500 m step for a control without edges is a fallback, not a scientific partition. Edges are set in the registry before any result is seen and must not be chosen after seeing results.

## Candidate mechanisms (never rules)

### `snow_water_to_next_year_leaves`: snow to melt to soil water to BTRAN, which acts on both transpiration and GPP, with GPP carried over to next year's leaf carbon only under conditions
Links: H2OSNO to QSNOMELT (holds when: a seasonal snowpack forms in the cell); QSNOMELT to H2OSOI (holds when: the soil is not frozen or already full when the melt comes, so the melt is not lost as runoff); H2OSOI to BTRAN (holds when: the root zone dries far enough in the growing season to lower BTRAN below 1); BTRAN to QVEGT (holds when: the canopy has leaves to transpire through); BTRAN to GPP (holds when: light, temperature, nitrogen or leaf area do not limit photosynthesis first in the same weeks); GPP to LEAFC (conditional carry-over) (holds when: the model allocates part of the new carbon to leaves, or to storage that feeds next year's leaves, rather than to stems and roots; the plants store carbon and draw on it the next year; leaves turn over slowly enough, or are grown anew from stored carbon, for this year's carbon to reach next year's leaves; the vegetation type sets the path: an evergreen canopy keeps its leaves, a deciduous one grows them anew; no disturbance (fire, mortality) removes the plants in between)
Competing: summer rain refills the soil and breaks the link from snow to soil water (RAIN, H2OSOI); warmer, drier air or more sunshine changes photosynthesis and transpiration without a change in soil water (TBOT, RH2M, FSDS); nitrogen limits photosynthesis whatever the water (FPG); a change in leaf area (fire, a previous dry year) changes transpiration and photosynthesis directly (TLAI, FAREA_BURNED); the plants' stored carbon buffers next year's leaves, so leaf carbon need not follow this year's GPP (LEAFC, TOTVEGC)
Methods: snow_peak, snow_gone, anomaly, difference, growth_peak, overlap, elevation_split.

### `temperature_to_heterotrophic_respiration`: temperature to soil temperature to heterotrophic respiration
Links: TSA to TSOI_10CM (holds when: no deep snow insulates the soil, which holds it near freezing); TSOI_10CM to HR (holds when: the soil is not frozen; the soil is neither so dry nor so wet that water limits decomposition)
Competing: soil water limits decomposition in a dry period, so a warm, dry spell can lower heterotrophic respiration (H2OSOI, SOILWATER_10CM); the size of the litter and soil carbon pools sets how much there is to decompose (TOTLITC, TOTSOMC, CWDC); nitrogen limits the decomposers' immobilization (FPI, SMINN); a fire adds or removes litter (COL_FIRE_CLOSS)
Methods: anomaly, difference, overlap, elevation_split, screen.

### `nitrogen_to_gpp`: nitrogen (soil mineral nitrogen and FPG) to GPP, in a run whose verified configuration leaves nitrogen as the only nutrient limit
Links: SMINN to FPG (holds when: the run cycles nitrogen and does not supplement it (suplnitro = 'NONE'), as in the verified configuration); FPG to GPP (holds when: only nitrogen can limit growth by nutrients: in the verified configuration the model represents phosphorus limitation, but phosphorus is supplemented to meet plant demand everywhere (suplphos = 'ALL'), which switches it off (FPG_P, the matching fraction for phosphorus in the monthly output, was exactly 1 in every cell and month of 2015-2023 in the Naches control run, checked on 2026-11-05: 160,728 finite cell-months a year), so nitrogen and phosphorus cannot co-limit; in a run without the phosphorus supplement, phosphorus can limit too, alone or with nitrogen, and FPG alone does not show the nutrient limit: FPG and FPG_P are needed together to tell nitrogen limitation from phosphorus limitation)
Competing: the plants' demand changes (more or less potential photosynthesis) and moves FPG without a change in supply (PLANT_NDEMAND); phosphorus limits GPP, alone or together with nitrogen; this cannot happen in the verified configuration, where the supplement (suplphos = 'ALL') switches phosphorus limitation off and FPG_P, in the monthly output, was exactly 1 in every cell and month of 2015-2023 in the Naches control run (checked on 2026-11-05: 160,728 finite cell-months a year); in a run without the supplement, FPG and FPG_P together tell the two apart (FPG); water stress limits GPP separately from nutrients, through BTRAN, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (BTRAN); temperature limits GPP separately from nutrients, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (TSA); light limits GPP separately from nutrients, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (FSDS); leaf area limits GPP separately from nutrients, and shows in neither FPG nor FPG_P (TLAI); a fire releases nitrogen or removes the plants that take it up (COL_FIRE_CLOSS, FAREA_BURNED)
Methods: anomaly, difference, overlap, screen.

### `burned_area_to_transpiration`: burned area to carbon lost to fire to leaf area to transpiration
Links: FAREA_BURNED to COL_FIRE_CLOSS (holds when: fire in the cell in the period: FAREA_BURNED above zero); COL_FIRE_CLOSS to TLAI (holds when: the fire burns leaves or whole plants, not only litter); TLAI to QVEGT (holds when: after the fire and before the canopy regrows)
Competing: drought lowers leaf area and transpiration in the same years that fires burn (BTRAN, H2OSOI, RAIN); the model's background fire burns a little in most periods, spread thin over many cells (FAREA_BURNED); different weather, not the fire, changes transpiration (TBOT, RH2M, FSDS)
Methods: screen, anomaly, difference, overlap, elevation_split.
