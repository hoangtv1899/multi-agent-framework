Six Studies, One Pipeline

The newest completed run of each kind the framework can do — a real basin, a controlled sweep, and a model-to-model coupling, for each of ELM (land surface) and PFLOTRAN (variably saturated subsurface). Every study began as one plain-language sentence; every figure below was drawn by the pipeline itself from that run's own record, and every verdict is the Analyzer's.

1 · ELM · real basin verdict: sufficient
elm_run_20260814_121346Naches, WA (HUC8 17030002)197919 columns · 471–1913 m

"How does the partitioning between surface runoff and recharge vary with elevation in the Naches sub-watershed for 1979 — and compare with observations?"

  • Where drainage happens it dominates: up to 0.84 of precipitation leaves as sub-surface drainage at the highest column (1913 m). Surface runoff is minor everywhere and does not follow elevation.
  • Several columns recharge without draining — e.g. 805 mm of recharge with zero drainage — because the inherited water table sits below the 3.8 m active soil in every column (median 12.1 m).
  • Elevation sets timing: at 1913 m snowmelt peaks in May (551 mm), drainage one month later (338 mm in June).
  • Against the two snow pillows the model runs low by ≈86 mm of snow water (NSE 0.29, KGE 0.46).
take-awayElevation controls when water moves; the inherited water-table depth controls which columns can drain at all. The warm start is not a detail — it is the switch.
2 · ELM · controlled sweep verdict: sufficient
elm_fixture_202608160905no place — one forcing cell1995–2004clay 5% vs 55% · as-is and doubled rain

"Does soil texture change the runoff/drainage split once the column has wetted up?"

  • Texture moves the runoff share only from 0.19 (clay 5%) to 0.23 (clay 55%); annual runoff itself is nearly identical (177 vs 186 mm/yr).
  • The clearest texture effect is timing, not amount: April drainage is 194 mm under clay 55% vs 38 mm under clay 5%.
  • Year-to-year weather moves the runoff share more (0.15–0.28 within one texture) than texture does.
take-awayIn a clean two-texture experiment, soil texture mostly changes when water leaves the column, hardly whether it runs off — and ordinary weather variability is the larger signal.
3 · PFLOTRAN · real basin verdict: sufficient
pflotran_run_20260818_195531Brandywine-Christina, PA/DE (HUC8 02040205)201015 columns

"How deep does water move through the unsaturated soil in the Brandywine-Christina basin in 2010?"

Clock note: plot times are simulation years. Each column first runs 10 years under 2010's mean rain so the profile can settle from its hydrostatic start, then calendar 2010 runs day by day as year 10–11 — so t = 11 y is 31 Dec 2010, and the snapshots at 10.25/10.5/10.75/11 are its quarters.

  • 12 of 15 columns have their water table at 0.1 m — the CONUS2 state saturates lowland Brandywine, leaving essentially no unsaturated soil to study there.
  • The three upland columns hold 6.3, 13.0 and 20.6 m of unsaturated depth and barely change: their profiles stay put across snapshots, and the water table moves at most 0.15 m in any column within the year.
  • Against the wells, the ensemble runs wet (bias −3.5 to −13.9 m) — the modelled starting state, not the year's weather, sets the answer.
take-awayThe question is answered by the initial condition more than the forcing: where CONUS2 says "already wet", there is nothing to move through — and the three dry columns are nearly static over a single year.
4 · PFLOTRAN · controlled sweep verdict: sufficient
pflotran_run_20260818_220421no place — one loam columnwater table at 2, 5, 10, 20 mone year of seasonal rain

"In a single loam soil column, how does the depth of the water table change how far one year of seasonal rain reaches?"

Same clock as slide 3: ten settling years of mean rain, then the rain year as year 10–11 — which is why the honest baseline is t = 10.25 y, not t = 0.

  • Measured against the right baseline (the start of the rain year), the rain changes saturation by at most 0.021–0.023, and only the top 0.75 m responds — the same answer whether the water table is at 2, 5 or 10 m.
  • Measured against t=0 the change looks up to 20× larger (0.23–0.44) — that is the model still adjusting from its initial state, not rain.
  • The solved water table stays within a few centimetres (0.013–0.066 m) of the depth it was prescribed — the experiment holds its own control.
take-awayOne year of rain wets less than a metre of loam no matter where the water table sits — and without the two-baseline discipline, spin-up would have been reported as rainfall response.
5 · one-way coupling · ELM → PFLOTRAN verdict: sufficient
pflotran_run_20260819_074628Brandywine-Christina20103 columns, reused verbatim from the ELM run

"Drive PFLOTRAN with the sub-surface drainage (QDRAI) from the prior ELM run — reuse its columns exactly, anchor each at ELM's own solved water table."

  • The lowland column (water table 4.25 m) felt ELM's drainage: the changed zone reached 2.8 m deep in the first half of the year and stood at 2.3 m at year end.
  • The two upland columns, whose ELM sent essentially zero drainage, changed saturation by at most 0.0000033 — nothing, correctly.
  • PFLOTRAN's solved water table lands within ≈0.5 m of ELM's anchor in every column.
take-awayThe handoff is physical end to end — the wet column answers, the dry ones stay silent. What one pass cannot do is repair the inherited deep state; that is exactly the job of the loop on the next slide.
6 · two-way coupling · ELM ↔ PFLOTRAN 6 rounds · each leg's verdict: sufficient
one SLURM job (773722) · sbatch once, both models insideBrandywine-Christina · 2010tolerance 0.10 m on movement between rounds

"Iterate: ELM's drainage drives PFLOTRAN; PFLOTRAN's solved water table becomes ELM's next starting state — until the water table stops moving."

  • Six full rounds inside a single 1 h 15 m allocation — ELM ensembles run in place, PFLOTRAN inline, a convergence check between every round.
  • Round 2 delivered the big correction: 18.5 m at the deep columns. The shallow column then settled at 0.002 m per round — converged.
  • The deep columns move a constant ≈0.50 m every round (0.504, 0.504, 0.505, 0.505): a steady physical drift — ELM sends them no drainage, PFLOTRAN keeps draining — not a shrinking numerical error.
  • Every ELM return leg was read by the Analyzer with no special casing: it found the coupling facts on the columns, drew the drift, and reported ELM holding within ≈1 m of the imposed water table.
take-awayThe coupling machinery is proven — one job, six rounds, honest verdicts throughout. What remains is a science question, not a plumbing one: the deep columns drift 0.5 m per round toward a much deeper balance, so "converged" there means choosing a spin-up length or a per-column tolerance.
